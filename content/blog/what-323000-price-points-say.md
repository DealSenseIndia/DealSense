DealSense currently holds **323,751 dated price readings** across 2,325 listings and 2,121 products. Not estimates, not scraped summaries of somebody else's chart — individual prices, each with the date it was seen.

That is enough data to answer a question every shopper asks, and almost every shop answers dishonestly: **is this actually a good price?**

## What the numbers look like today

Of the 2,165 listings with at least ten readings:

- **1,000 (46%) are priced below their own median.** That sounds like a permanent sale. It mostly is not: prices drift downward over a product's life, and the median includes the months when it cost more.
- **384 (17.7%) are at their recorded low** — within 1% of the cheapest reading we hold for that listing.

So roughly one in six products is genuinely at the bottom of its record today. That is the useful number. "46% off" is not.

## The trap inside "lowest price ever"

A record is only as good as what is in it. Ours had junk.

While checking the catalogue we found a ₹5,699 air fryer carrying readings of **₹5,49,900** — a hundred times its real price — alongside a ₹824 perfume with ₹9 readings and an ₹11,299 SSD with ₹230 readings. Sixty-five points across 34 listings were off by more than ten times the product's own median. They were import errors, not prices.

All 65 are gone, logged, and the affected charts were rebuilt from what remains. The [air fryer's record](/product/glen-digital-air-fryer-6l-1500w-temperature-timer-control-7-preset-men-28406) now runs from ₹4,396 to ₹8,995, which is what an air fryer costs.

This matters more than it sounds. If one absurd point survives, every average, every "lowest ever" badge and every verdict on that page is built on it.

## A phone at its most expensive point

The clearest example of why medians beat adjectives: the [iPhone 17 (White, 256 GB)](/product/apple-iphone-17-white-256-gb-26383) has **512 readings** in our record. Its cheapest was **₹58,030 on 25 July 2026**. Its current price, **₹82,900**, is also its highest reading and its median.

A shop can call that "₹17,000 off MRP" and be technically right. Against its own history, the phone is at the top of its range. The honest answer is: not now.

## How to read any price on this site

Three numbers, in this order:

1. **Current price against the median.** Above it, you are paying more than the product's typical price.
2. **Current price against the recorded low, and the date of that low.** A low from four months ago that lasted one day is a weaker signal than a low you can buy at today.
3. **How many readings the record holds.** Ten points is thin. Five hundred is a pattern.

The [price history page](/price-history) lists the products with the deepest records, and every [category](/categories) page shows how many dated prices sit behind each product.

None of this guarantees tomorrow's price. It does mean you can tell a real drop from a discount that only exists in the marketing.
