The [iPhone 17 (White, 256 GB)](/product/apple-iphone-17-white-256-gb-26383) is the most-tracked phone in our catalogue: **512 dated readings**, from 10 September 2025 to 18 September 2026. That is enough record to say something precise instead of "prices may vary".

## What the record holds

- **Most readings: ₹82,900.** That is the launch price, the highest reading, the current price — and the median. More than half the record sits at exactly this number.
- **A dip to ₹74,900** on 22 April 2026.
- **One reading at ₹58,030** on 25 July 2026. Nothing else in the record comes within 2% of it.

So the phone has spent most of a year at its ceiling, with two moments below it. The first lasted long enough to be believable. The second is a single point.

## Why one cheap day is not a floor

A single reading at ₹58,030 tells you a price existed for at least one day. It does not tell you it was buyable: stock can vanish in hours, and a listing can show a price that checkout never honours.

This is why we show the **date** next to every point and why "lowest recorded" always means *lowest we have seen*, not *price you can get*. A record with one outlier is a weaker signal than a record with a month of readings in the same band.

## The verdict today

At ₹82,900 the phone is at the top of its own range. Against MRP the discount looks large; against its own record it is the worst price in a year of readings.

What would change that:

- a reading near ₹74,900 that holds for more than a few days;
- a price under ₹70,000, which would be below every reading except one;
- a newer model launch, which usually pulls the previous generation down within weeks.

Until one of those shows up, the honest answer is wait. The [smartphones category](/categories/smartphones) lists every phone we track, each with the number of dated prices behind it, and the [price history page](/price-history) shows the products with the deepest records.
