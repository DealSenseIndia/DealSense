A home gym fails for a boring reason: people buy the bench first, then discover they have no floor space, no mat, and no plan for the days they do not want to lift. The useful way to furnish one is to list the jobs — weights, floor, resistance, cardio, grip, recovery, hydration, carrying — and fill each job with the cheapest thing that does it well enough.

We did that with prices we track. The plan below fills **all ten slots for ₹9,607**, against a ₹15,000 budget.

## The decisions worth explaining

**Adjustable dumbbells before a bench.** A pair of adjustable dumbbells covers presses, rows, squats and carries in about a square metre. A bench without weights is a very expensive stool.

**Bands are not a downgrade.** They are the cheapest way to add pull work — the one pattern dumbbells handle badly at home — and they pack into the duffle.

**The massage gun is the one indulgence.** At ₹693 it is not a medical device and we do not pretend it is. It is a convenience for the day after legs.

**The shoes are optional.** At ₹2,397 they are the single most expensive line. If you already own a pair of trainers, the plan drops to ₹7,210 and nothing else changes.

**A watch is not a gym requirement.** It is here because the slot exists for people who track sessions. If you use your phone, skip it.

## What we are not claiming

We do not test grip, durability, motor noise or whether a mat curls at the edges. This is a **price-led build**: for each job, the cheapest tracked listing that fits the slot and has recorded history behind its price. If you want a review of a specific dumbbell's knurling, we are the wrong site — and we would rather say so than invent one.

What we can tell you is what each item costs, what it has cost, and whether today is a sensible day to buy it. That is the part most shopping articles leave out.
