A "70% off" badge is not information. It is an arithmetic trick played on a number the seller chose. The only number that can tell you whether today is a good day to buy is the price this product actually sold at, over time.

That is the whole idea behind DealSense, and it is worth learning to do yourself even if you never open our site again.

## One: ignore the MRP, find the median

List price is whatever the seller wants the discount to look large against. The useful question is: **what does this normally cost?** Take every recorded price for the product, line them up, and look at the middle value — the median. If today's price is below the median, the discount is real. If it is above, the badge is decoration.

Open any product on DealSense — say this [INALSA 3L air fryer](/product/inalsa-air-fryer-3l-non-toxic-digital-air-fryer-air-crisp-technology-29460) — and the chart draws the recorded readings. The median and the all-time low sit next to the current price, computed from that same series.

## Two: check the all-time low, not the "was" price

A product at ₹8,652 is not a bargain because it once carried a ₹15,000 sticker. It is a bargain if ₹8,652 is close to the lowest it has genuinely been recorded at.

The [ViewSonic 27-inch 240Hz monitor](/product/viewsonic-vx2779a-hd-pro-27-inch-68-5-cm-fhd-ips-gaming-monitor-240hz--1789) is a useful example: a gaming monitor is exactly the kind of product where list prices are inflated and "deals" are permanent. The recorded low is the only honest floor.

## Three: look at the date of the newest price

Every price on the internet is a claim with a timestamp. Ours is no different. A price recorded four days ago could have moved yesterday — so each product page says when its newest reading was taken, and warns you when it is old.

If a site will not tell you when it last checked, assume it has not.

## What we refuse to do

We do not predict the next price. We do not invent a "typical price" when the history is thin. And we do not print a verdict when there are fewer than three recorded points, because a line made of two dots can say anything.

That is also why some products show a straight "insufficient history" label instead of a score. It is less exciting than a percentage badge. It is also the truth.

## Ninety seconds, applied

1. Find the median of the recorded prices.
2. Compare today with the all-time low, not with MRP.
3. Read the date on the newest reading.
4. If the deal is real, buy. If it is not, leave it — there is another drop coming, and now you will see it.
